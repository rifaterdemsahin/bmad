# Stage B: Creating a Shared Multi-Author Publication on Substack

Creating a shared or multi-author publication on Substack is a fantastic way to distribute the workload and bring diverse voices into one space. Substack handles this smoothly by letting the publication owner invite collaborators with varying levels of access.

Here is the step-by-step process to set up your shared publication and invite your team.

---

## Step 1: Create the Main Publication

If you haven't started the newsletter yet, you (or one designated team member) need to create the foundation first.

1. Log into Substack and click on your **Profile icon** in the top right corner.
2. Select **Settings** from the drop-down menu.
3. Scroll down to the **Publications** section and click **Create another publication** (or just follow the initial setup if you are completely new to Substack).
4. Choose your URL, publication name, and complete the basic setup steps.

---

## Step 2: Invite Your Co-Authors / Collaborators

Once the publication is active, the owner can invite the rest of the team:

1. Go to your Substack **Dashboard**.
2. Click on **Settings** in the top navigation bar.
3. On the left-hand menu, click on **Team**.
4. Click the **Invite** button.
5. Enter the email address of your co-author and click **Send invite**.

---

## Step 3: Define Their Roles

When you invite someone, you can click on their assigned role to adjust what they can see and do. Substack offers three primary levels of access:

| Role | What They Can Do | Best For |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Admin** | Full access to settings, stats, subscriber details, billing, and the ability to publish or delete posts. | Co-founders or trusted managing editors. |
| **Contributor** | Can view, edit, and publish draft posts. They **cannot** access publication settings, financial data, or overall subscriber stats. | Regular writers, editors, or columnists. |
| **Byline** | Strictly listed as an author on the publication (like on the "About" page) but has **no access** to the backend dashboard or drafts. | One-time guest writers or quiet partners. |

> **Quick Tip on Visibility:** You can toggle a team member's status between **Public** and **Private**. If set to Public, they will automatically appear on your publication's "About" and "People" pages.

---

## Step 4: Adding Multiple Authors to a Single Post

When it comes time to publish a piece that was a joint effort, you can easily add multiple bylines to a single draft:

1. Open your draft post.
2. Click the **"+" button** next to your name at the top under the title.
3. Select the collaborator(s) you want to include. Their profiles and avatars will now appear together at the top of the article.

---

## Important Note on Monetization

If you plan to turn on paid subscriptions for your shared publication, keep in mind that **Substack only allows you to connect a single Stripe account** to the publication. Substack does not automatically split payouts. The primary account owner will receive 100% of the revenue (minus Substack's 10% and Stripe fees) and will be responsible for manually distributing the earnings to the rest of the team based on whatever internal agreement you all decide on.
